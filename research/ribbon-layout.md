# Ribbon, stripe and supergraphic placement

Sources give the principles. The numbers (degrees, px, %) are my buildable translations, marked **[rule]**. Tune them by eye.

## 1. Composition
- Layout elements override default scan patterns. Z/F paths describe graphic-light pages, so place the flow deliberately (Smashing). Lines act as direction cues, and a line perpendicular to the eye's travel is a barrier that stops it (Smashing).
- Use diagonals as the eye path. Smashing notes implied diagonals move the eye between anchors. Horizontal ribbons run perpendicular to scroll and read as barriers, which matches your "flat" feedback.
- **[rule]** Use 2 angles only, roughly -12 deg and +12 deg (range 8-18), alternating section to section for a zig-zag down the page. No horizontals. Add curvature to a diagonal rather than a third angle.
- **[rule]** Ribbons enter from one side and exit through the opposite edge or the bottom/top edge, so they never end inside the page. Keep a ribbon's visible band 30-45% of viewport height, offset to the outer third of the viewport (asymmetry, rule of thirds) and away from the center column where content sits.
- Asymmetry reads as energetic and youthful, and whitespace gives the product weight (WebsiteBuilderExpert). Put the ribbon's heavy end opposite the section's product or headline.
- **Bleed:** a decisive crop reads as continuation. A sliver or an edge landing exactly on the frame edge creates tension (Daily Photo Tips, tangent-at-edge warnings). **[rule]** Bleed at least 10-15% of ribbon length past the viewport edge, or clip fully off-canvas. Never leave 0-8% of a band or a rounded end visible.

## 2. Tangents and conflicts
- Near-misses are worse than clear differences: snap exactly or separate clearly (MadeGood). Parallel edges repeat and tightly spaced lines create tension (3D Artist). Overlap is fine if you offset aligned edges.
- **[rule]** Ribbons never run within 20 deg of parallel to the rainbow divider unless 150px or more apart. Better: keep the divider horizontal and ribbons diagonal, so they cross decisively instead of nearly touching.
- **[rule]** Keep at least 150px between any ribbon edge and a divider, and never show a ribbon and a divider in the same viewport band (no ribbon within one screen height of a divider unless it crosses it at 30 deg or more).
- **[rule]** Clear zones: 24px around buttons, 32px around headings/body text, 24px around product tiles. Decoration is z-index below content and never crosses text. Avoid ribbon edges falling within 8-24px of a tile, heading or button edge; either cross decisively or stay well clear.
- Space decorations about 1.5x the band thickness from each other.

## 3. Hierarchy and density
- Hierarchy comes from contrast with surroundings. Saturation draws the eye, so give it to important items, and avoid many similar-value, similar-saturation colors (NN/g). Large neutral or low-saturation areas keep balance.
- **[rule]** Ribbons are the lowest-chroma layer: pastel tints at 35-55% of tile color saturation, lightness within about 10-12% of white-page value. Product tile shapes and photos carry the bold color.
- NN/g caps large elements at 2 and contrast treatments at about 3. **[rule]** Per viewport: max 1 ribbon, optionally 1 divider, and 0 big blobs next to a product photo. Blobs go behind tiles only, inside the tile, at 60% or less of tile area.
- **[rule]** Consistency: one stroke width per stripe (e.g. 14px at desktop, 3 or 4 stripes with 6px gaps), one palette of 4-6 tints shared by ribbons and divider. Vary only angle, position and length. The divider's 3 stripes should reuse ribbon hues.
- Squint test: blur the page, and the products and headings must dominate (NN/g).

## 4. Responsive
- On small screens remove what adds nothing, simplify and crop to the key detail, and avoid text over images (NN/g, mobile imagery).
- **[rule]** Phones: 1 ribbon per 2-3 screens, thinner (about 60% of desktop width), 3 stripes max, angle steepened to 15-20 deg so it crosses the narrow width, hugging a screen edge. Keep ribbons out of text columns entirely. Drop blobs. Keep the divider at 2 stripes if needed.

## 5. 70s references
- Supergraphics: oversized color bands flowing ceiling to wall to floor, "King Kong-sized curving stripes" looping across walls, routing around or running across openings (Click Americana, CS Monitor). Barbara Stauffacher Solomon's Sea Ranch graphics (1967) merged Swiss modernism with bold California pop, painted at wall scale (Design Milk).
- Stepped stripes: Verner Panton's 1971 concentric stripes and Saul Bass's Paul Harris Stores logo; Aaron Draplin revived it with a modern touch (GDUSA).
- Polaroid's rainbow stripe, by Paul Giambarba from the 1960s, is one shared element varied per product line (It's Nice That).
- Contemporary: Aviator Nation uses bold stripes as its hero motif (Ecommerce Fastlane). Pizza Express uses stripes as a flexible anchor across touchpoints (Marketing Week).
- Keep retro clean with few retro elements on a restrained base, 1-3 vintage colors on white (Ecommerce Fastlane).
- Note: no source gave rounded-corner stripe rules; "turn the corner" is your design choice. **[rule]** Use corner radius of at least 2x the band width.
